# Energy as a Relation-Level Test

## Established physics boundary

Energy is a conserved physical quantity in the appropriate closed-system accounting. In electromagnetism, Maxwell's equations lead to the Poynting theorem, which relates the rate of change of field energy to energy flux and work on currents. NIST gives the standard field-energy density as

`u = 1/2 (ε₀ E² + B²/μ₀)`

and the Poynting flux as

`S = E × H`.

For a source-free volume, changes in stored field energy are balanced by flux through the boundary. With currents, the `J·E` term accounts for transfer between field and matter.

## Ω / Relation question

The research question is narrower than "energy is a relation".

We test whether a relation-first model can generate a consistent hierarchy:

`state → local relation → dynamics → stored energy → energy transfer → balance`

without inserting a separate energy mechanism by hand for every domain.

## LIGHT mapping

For the scalar relation test:

`r_i = q_{i+1} - q_i`

and

`E_relation ∝ Σ r_i²`.

The same local relation appears in the equation of motion and in the stored-energy term.

For electromagnetism, the analogous target is not a scalar copy of the chain. The fields are vector fields and obey Maxwell constraints. The test must therefore preserve vector structure, transverse modes, boundary conditions, and the electromagnetic energy/flux equations.

## Falsification condition

The relation hypothesis is weakened or rejected as a universal architecture if energy conservation/transfer can only be reproduced by introducing unrelated, domain-specific machinery that is not derivable from the frozen relation schema.

A successful result must include quantitative error bounds and explicit boundary accounting.

## Status

`HYPOTHESIS / TESTABLE`

REL-01E provides a first calculated result: the minimal relation model supports both propagation and a stable energy balance in the tested closed chain. This is supportive evidence for the computational architecture, not evidence that all physical energy is fundamentally relational.
